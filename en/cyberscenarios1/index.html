Welcome to the Scenario

In this interactive scenario, you are a Cybersecurity Engineer at COLHAK Corporation. Your mission is to analyze an evolving security threat, make critical decisions, and protect the company from potential disaster. Carefully evaluate the information presented at each step. Are you ready?

Simulation Roles

  • Your Role: Cybersecurity Engineer - You are part of the COLHAK Corporation cybersecurity team. You will take an active role in incident response, perform analysis, and make critical decisions.
  • Ezgi (Tier-1 SOC Analyst) - The eyes and ears of your team; detecting initial alerts and suspicious activities.
  • Alper (Tier-2 Analyst) - The expert who deeply analyzes incidents escalated by Ezgi and conducts technical investigations.
  • Ceyda (Cybersecurity Manager) - The leader of your cybersecurity team, making final decisions during crisis moments and providing strategic guidance.
  • Sales Director - A critical stakeholder responsible for business operations; absolutely avoids disruptions to business processes like live demos.
  • Finance Director (CFO) - The senior executive responsible for the company's financial health.

🕒 07:15 – First Signal: Enriched SIEM Alert

At the start of the day, Tier-1 SOC Analyst Ezgi notices a critical alert on the SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) dashboard. A correlation rule has been triggered: "Periodic Data Exfiltration from Critical Asset Network to Unknown C2 Server".

ALERT ID: 8451
TRIGGER: Correlation Rule - "Suspicious C2 Beaconing"
SOURCE IP: 10.10.30.15 (VLAN: IoT Devices)
HOSTNAME: CAMERA-3FLOOR-OCEAN
SOURCE MAC: 00:1A:2B:3C:4D:5E
DESTINATION IP: 88.54.12.219 (Reputation: Low, Category: Unknown)
PROTOCOL: UDP/443
OBSERVATION: A 128KB data packet is sent every 5 minutes for the last 3 hours.
PCAP SUMMARY: DNS query for 'update.serve-config.net' resolved to 88.54.12.219. UDP payload contains obfuscated Base64 strings.
MITRE ATT&ACK MAPPING: T1041 (Exfiltration Over C2), T1571 (Non-Standard Port)
Ezgi: "This isn't normal traffic. Source IP appears in the IoT VLAN and the destination is a suspicious address we haven't seen before. The MITRE matches are also concerning. I'm escalating this to Tier-2 (Alper) immediately."

🕒 07:30 – Simultaneous Developments and First Decision Point

Almost simultaneously with Ezgi's analysis, two different incident logs drop into the system. As a team, you must decide which event to prioritize.

Incident 1: Help Desk Ticket #7254

User: Ayse Yilmaz

Subject: The camera in the 3rd Floor Ocean Meeting Room keeps freezing and the web interface is very slow. There was no such problem yesterday morning.

Incident 2: Critical System Alert #9112 (Red Herring)

System: Active Directory

Subject: Over 50 failed login attempts detected on the Finance Director's (CFO) account within the last 15 minutes.

Team Leader: "We have two potentially serious incidents. One could be potential data leakage, the other an attack on a senior executive's account. Our resources are limited, which one do we focus on?"

🕒 07:50 – Incorrect Prioritization

The team decides to focus on the Finance Director's account. Rapid investigation reveals that the CFO is on vacation abroad and forgot their new password, causing multiple failed login attempts from their mobile device. The account is temporarily locked, and the situation is contained.

Alper: "This was a 'false positive'. But we lost about 20 minutes investigating this. Now we need to urgently return to the other incident. I hope we're not too late."

🧑‍💻 08:00 – Alper's Deep Analysis

Tier-2 Analyst Alper combines the SIEM alert with the help desk ticket. He queries the suspicious `10.10.30.15` IP address in the company's Asset Management System. The result is clear: The IP belongs to a "SmartEye 3000" model IP camera in the 3rd Floor Ocean Meeting Room.

A quick internet search reveals a recently disclosed critical vulnerability (CVE-2025-1337) in this model's firmware. This vulnerability allows unauthorized Remote Code Execution (RCE).

Alper: "Okay, now everything fits. The slowness in the camera isn't a malfunction, it's a symptom. The device has been compromised via a known vulnerability and is sending data to a C2 server as part of a botnet. We need to determine the next step."

🕵️‍♂️ 08:15 – Deepening Investigation: What is the Enemy's Intent?

Alper's findings are clear: The device is compromised. But what is the enemy's real goal? Is it just a DDoS botnet, or the first step of a larger plan? Ceyda turns to you to decide the next step.

Ceyda: "We found the entry point, good job. Now we need to understand what they are after. We have three options: Investigate outside, focus inside, or check for spread?"

Option A: Analyze C2 Server

Investigate the target IP and domain on Threat Intelligence platforms (VirusTotal, Shodan, etc.). This can provide info on the attacker's identity and TTPs (Tactics, Techniques, Procedures).

Option B: Analyze Payload

Analyze the 'payload.sh' script and 'c2.pl' Perl script downloaded to the device in a sandbox. Learn exactly what the malware does and its capabilities (e.g., network scanning).

Option C: Perform Internal Network Scan

Start an emergency scan to find other "SmartEye 3000" cameras on the network. Understand how widespread the vulnerability is and identify other potential victims.

🕒 08:30 – Sudden Complication: Management Pressure

Just as your analysis is concluding, that expected call comes from the Sales Director. Her voice is quite tense.

Sales Director: "Is your team messing with the camera in the Ocean Room? That camera needs to be working in 1 hour; we are doing a live demo presentation for a very important foreign client! You cannot close the room, find a solution immediately!"

This new information reveals that the decision to be made has not only a technical but also an operational dimension. The pressure on the team has increased.

🏢 08:45 – Crisis Meeting and Final Decision

You summarize the situation to Cybersecurity Manager Ceyda: "We have an IP camera compromised using the CVE-2025-1337 vulnerability. It's not just communicating externally, but also acting according to our initial findings. We haven't detected lateral movement within the network yet. However, the Sales Department needs that camera in less than 1 hour."

Ceyda: "Understood. We are short on time and under pressure. We seem to have three options. The decision is yours. What do we do?"

Make your decision as a team:

Option A: Isolate and Patch (Safe Route)

Immediately disconnect the camera from the network. Take the device to the lab, capture its image, and update with the latest firmware. The threat is stopped instantly.

Risk: We can't learn more about the attacker's ultimate goal. The sales presentation will be cancelled.

Option B: Divert to Honeypot (Risky Observation)

Redirect the camera's traffic to a "Honeypot" environment completely isolated from the main network. Collect valuable intelligence by secretly watching the attacker's behaviors (TTPs).

Risk: The attacker might notice the Honeypot or launch a more aggressive attack (e.g., ransomware) during observation.

Option C: Quarantine and Observe (Calculated Middle Ground)

Move the camera to an isolated VLAN with no internet access. Block the device's internet exit but observe what it tries to do to 'bait' assets on the local network. This might reveal lateral movement attempts.

Risk: A misconfiguration could allow the attacker to escape quarantine. The sales presentation might still be delayed.

📞 09:00 – Communication Crisis: Stakeholder Management

While your technical intervention decision is being implemented, Ceyda comes to you. She looks worried. "The technical side seems under control, but my phone won't stop ringing. The Sales Director is asking about the fate of the demo, and the CFO wants a report on potential financial impact. How do we manage the situation internally? What should be our official position?"

Ceyda: "We are at a moment as important as the technical intervention. The advice you give will determine how the company perceives this crisis internally. What do we say?"

Option A: Technical Focus

Say 'My priority is to stop this threat, communication is your job' and focus only on the technical operation. Let management resolve the situation among themselves.

Option B: Full Transparency

Inform the Sales Director that the demo is definitely cancelled due to a critical cybersecurity incident. Explain the situation to the CFO in full detail and ensure they are prepared for the worst-case scenario.

Option C: Controlled Information Flow

Tell Sales there is a 'temporary network issue' and you are trying to resolve it. Report the situation to the CFO as an 'incident under control' to avoid creating panic.
