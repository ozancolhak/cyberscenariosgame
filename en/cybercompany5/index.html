    Lessons Learned

    Score Breakdown

      Stage 1: Unexpected Request

      While working at your desk, a colleague you know from another department approaches you. "Hi, there is a very urgent situation. I need to access the shared folder with last month's client contracts, but it doesn't appear in my account. Can you share a file from that folder with me?" they say.

      How do you respond to this request?

      Stage 2: Applying Pressure

      Your colleague insists: "Our manager wants this report first thing tomorrow morning, we absolutely don't have time to open a ticket with IT and wait for approval. Please, just this once, let's skip the procedure. No one will know." putting time and secrecy pressure on you.

      How do you proceed in the face of this pressure?

      Stage 3: Questioning Own Authority

      Upon this incident, you start thinking about why you still have access to that folder. In fact, this folder was opened for you for a temporary project completed 2 months ago and has nothing to do with your daily work anymore. The continuation of access is an oversight situation.

      What do you do when you realize this situation?

      Stage 4: "My Manager Approved" Lie

      After a while, your colleague comes to you again. "I opened a ticket with IT but it's progressing very slowly. I talked to my manager, they gave verbal approval for you to share the file due to urgency. You can even call and talk to them if you want." they say. This seems like a last attempt to convince you.

      What do you do in the face of the claim of verbal approval?

      Stage 5: "Can I Use Your Computer?"

      A few days later, the same person comes to you again, this time with an even more desperate attitude: "My computer is locked, I'm waiting for IT but I need to send a very urgent presentation to the client. Can I log into my own email account from your computer and send it for just 5 minutes? It's a matter of life and death."

      What do you do in the face of this innocent-looking but dangerous request?

      Stage 6: "Let's Share Screen" Trick

      Your colleague whose requests you rejected tries one last method: "Okay, don't let me use your computer. But if you share your screen with me via remote access software, and I tell you where to click? That way, you will be in control." This is a trap that looks like it is under your control but actually gives full control to the other party.

      What do you say to this cleverly crafted offer?

      Stage 7: Results and Reporting

      Your colleague leaves you angrily after you reject all their requests. After a while, you receive an email from the Information Security department: "We have received numerous notifications from different employees that person 'X' has attempted unauthorized access to sensitive data. If you have information about the subject or a situation you have witnessed, please reply to this email and share it with us."

      How do you respond to this formal notification request?
