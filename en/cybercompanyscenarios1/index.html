    Score Breakdown

      Stage 1: Email Arrives

      A high-priority email titled "IT Announcement: Company Password Update" lands in your inbox. The sender address appears as itdestek@firma-secure.com. The message states that you urgently need to renew your password due to security policies and says "Click Here to Update Your Password" on a large blue button in the middle.

      What do you do?

      Stage 2: Urgency Tactic

      When you read the email carefully, a sentence written in red and bold letters at the end of the text catches your attention: "ATTENTION: Accounts of users who do not update within 24 hours will be permanently locked for security reasons!" This statement puts pressure on you to act immediately.

      What is your attitude towards this time pressure?

      Stage 3: Suspicious Attachment

      At the bottom of the email, there is a Word document named "Instructions.doc" with the note "You can review the attached file for detailed password change instructions." This is presented as an alternative for those who do not want to click the link.

      What is your approach to the attached file?

      Stage 4: Examining Sender Address

      When you click on the sender or say show details, you notice that the real sender address behind the visible name itdestek@firma-secure.com is a completely irrelevant address like no-reply@secure-mail.info.

      What do you think about this inconsistency?

      Stage 5: Responsibility to Warn Others

      You are now sure this email is a dangerous phishing attack. However, this email was sent not just to you, but to the entire company. Other colleagues may not be as careful as you and could fall into the trap.

      What is your corporate responsibility in this situation?

      Stage 6: Attack Repeats

      The next day, another email titled "HR Announcement: Payroll Update" lands in your inbox. The sender, content structure, and "urgent" emphasis are almost identical to the password email from the previous day. Only the subject and request have been changed. The link says "Click to view your payroll".

      What do you do when encountering a similar method again?
